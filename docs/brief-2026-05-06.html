AI Omnibus Trilogue Confirmed for May 13 — 7 Days to Decisive Vote, August 2 Legally Operative

AI/ML Health Tech SaaS/B2B
iKey Finding
Multiple authoritative secondary sources confirm a follow-up political trilogue on the Digital Omnibus on AI is scheduled for approximately May 13, 2026, seven days from today, under the Cypriot Council Presidency. The April 28 second trilogue collapsed after roughly twelve hours over the conformity assessment architecture for AI embedded in Annex I regulated products (medical devices, machinery, toys). The August 2, 2026 high-risk deadline remains the only legally operative date: no delay has been formally adopted.
§Deep Analysis
This is a material update to omnibus-ai-trilogue-fail-2026-04-29-06. The May 13 session is the Cypriot Presidency's final realistic opportunity to close the file before its term ends June 30. Sources at Bird & Bird (published May 4) noted 'no date has been formally confirmed' in official channels, but DLA Piper, Modulos.ai, and olegov.substack all corroborate May 13. The single blocking issue is the Annex I conformity assessment architecture: whether AI embedded in regulated products (MDR, IVDR, Machinery Regulation) must additionally comply with AI Act conformity assessment, or whether sectoral product safety law is sufficient. This matters acutely for Health Tech clients whose AI-enabled medical devices sit in this intersection. Four scenarios remain live: (1) Deal closes May 13, Cypriot Presidency (~30% per analyst estimates); (2) Lithuanian Presidency closes in Q3, original August 2 passes without postponement legally in force (~25%); (3) Split deal — Annex I carved off, rest of Omnibus passes (~15%); (4) No deal before August 2 (~30%). In scenarios 2 and 4, the Commission may issue forbearance or transitional guidance, but this is speculative and not legally binding. The EDPB/EDPS Annual Report confirms the Board worked with the Commission on joint guidelines on the AI Act and EU data protection law interplay, targeted for adoption in 2026 — those guidelines are not contingent on Omnibus outcome and will proceed regardless. The Article 50 watermarking obligations and GPAI obligations (Articles 50-55) are also not in substantive dispute and apply August 2, 2026 regardless of Omnibus outcome. For SaaS/B2B clients deploying AI tools in EU contexts: if they have any systems that could be classified as Annex III high-risk (recruitment AI, credit scoring, biometric verification), August 2 is the working deadline. For Health Tech clients with AI-enabled medical devices: the Annex I question is existential — the Omnibus outcome directly determines their regulatory compliance architecture.

AI Act Article 50 Watermarking Code of Practice: Final Version Due Within Weeks, Working Groups Completing May 2026

AI/ML SaaS/B2B E-commerce
iKey Finding
The European Commission's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content (Article 50 AI Act) is in its final drafting stage, with the Commission's own website confirming a 'beginning of June' finalization target. Working group meetings are scheduled through May 2026. The second draft was published March 5, 2026. Article 50 transparency obligations apply August 2, 2026 regardless of Omnibus outcome.
§Deep Analysis
This is an update to the ongoing watermarking Code of Practice tracking item. The Commission's digital strategy page confirms the code is expected to be finalized 'by beginning of June this year' and that working group meetings run through May 2026. The second draft introduced a simplified two-layered marking approach (secured metadata plus watermarking), with optional fingerprinting/logging, and requires signatories to provide free, EU-localised, GDPR-compliant detection tools. Critically, adherence to the Code does not constitute conclusive evidence of compliance with Article 50 obligations — it is a benchmark, not a safe harbor. For SaaS and AI/ML clients: any platform that generates AI content (images, video, audio, text) for distribution in the EU must be Article 50-compliant by August 2. This is not deferred by the Omnibus — GPAI obligations including Article 50 were not in substantive dispute during trilogue. The second draft shifts some burden: GPAI model providers are now 'encouraged' rather than required to implement marking at model level, which increases burden on downstream system providers who cannot assume upstream models arrive with built-in watermarking. E-commerce clients using AI-generated product imagery, marketing copy, or synthetic audio are deployers under Article 50(4) and must label deepfakes and public interest text. The Code's 'editorial exemption' (human review with documented reviewer identity and approval date) is the primary compliance pathway for content that passes through editorial control.

3-Month Outlook

UK DUAA Mandatory Data Protection Complaints Regime: 44 Days to June 19 Deadline — Fresh Guidance Published Yesterday

SaaS/B2B E-commerce Health Tech
iKey Finding
The UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 mandatory data protection complaints procedure takes effect June 19, 2026 — 44 days away. From that date, every UK data controller must operate a formal, accessible complaints process as a statutory prerequisite before data subjects can escalate to the ICO. Sidley Austin published a detailed compliance guide on May 5, 2026 (yesterday). No exemptions apply; all UK-subject controllers are covered.
§Deep Analysis
This is a net-new item for this practice's tracking. The DUAA (which received Royal Assent June 19, 2025 and most provisions commenced February 5, 2026) introduces a structural change to the UK complaints landscape via Section 103, inserting new Section 164A into the DPA 2018. From June 19, 2026: (1) data subjects must first raise complaints with the controller before escalating to the ICO; (2) controllers must acknowledge complaints within 30 days; (3) controllers must investigate without undue delay and communicate outcomes; (4) complaints must be accepted through any channel, including social media and informal communications — not only designated forms. The ICO published final guidance on February 12, 2026 ('How to deal with data protection complaints'). Non-compliance exposes controllers to ICO fines of up to £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover under UK GDPR, plus reputational risk from ICO publication of enforcement actions. For SaaS/B2B clients with UK customer bases: this is an operational compliance item requiring immediate process review and privacy notice updates. For E-commerce clients: particularly acute given high volume of customer data interactions and the broad definition of 'complaint' (any expression of dissatisfaction about data handling). Note the EU adequacy risk dimension flagged in ongoing tracking: the DUAA's divergence from EU GDPR on ADM, cookie consent, and research creates ongoing EU adequacy review risk, though this complaints regime specifically is actually a consumer-rights strengthening measure that would not itself create adequacy tension.

This Month

Update client privacy notices to reference the right to complain directly to the controller — this must be in place before June 19.
03
Train client customer-facing teams to recognize and route data protection complaints from any channel (social media, verbal, informal).
04
For regulated clients (fintech, health): map DUAA complaints process against existing FCA or CQC complaints obligations to design an integrated workflow.
3-Month Outlook
01
Post-June 19: monitor ICO enforcement signals for complaints-handling failures — this is an easy enforcement target (process failures are visible and documentable).
02
Track DUAA secondary legislation: Section 164B gives Secretary of State power to require complaint volume reporting to ICO — if activated, creates new disclosure obligation.

Key Themes

Cross-sector patterns · 6 May 2026
01

The August 2 Wall: Regulators and Industry Are Converging on One Unmovable Date

Across all items today, a single date dominates: August 2, 2026. The AI Omnibus failure has not created regulatory confusion — it has clarified that August 2 is the floor, not the ceiling. Article 50 watermarking obligations apply August 2. High-risk AI system obligations apply August 2 absent a deal. The market is responding accordingly: law firm alerts published this week uniformly instruct clients to plan against the original deadline and treat Omnibus relief as upside only. This is a practice opportunity: clients who have been deferring AI compliance on Omnibus assumptions need to be brought back to baseline planning immediately.
02

Complaints-Handling as the New Compliance Frontier: UK DUAA Joins a Broader Pattern

The UK DUAA June 19 complaints regime joins a broader regulatory pattern of formalizing and individualizing data subject complaint rights at the controller level. This is not merely procedural — it creates documented evidence trails that regulators can use in enforcement, and it shifts the first line of accountability from regulators to controllers. For practices advising SaaS and E-commerce clients, this represents a shift from 'privacy notice compliance' to 'operational complaints infrastructure.' The 44-day runway is short for clients with no existing framework.
03

Israeli Regulatory Maturation: PPA Operating as a Quasi-Legislative Body

The PPA's February 2026 consent opinion, combined with the Chambers 2026 confirmation that PPA guidance is treated as binding by the Authority itself, signals that Israeli privacy regulation is entering a new phase. The PPA is no longer a registry-based regulator — it is an active enforcement agency issuing substantive guidance that it treats as statutory. For a practice with Israeli-operating clients, this requires monitoring PPA publications with the same rigor applied to EU DPA opinions.
